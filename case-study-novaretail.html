NovaRetail KPI Governance — Scoping two conflicting metrics

Before writing a single dbt model, the real problem was that Marketing and Finance didn't agree on what "revenue" and "loyal customer" meant. This is the functional scoping work that came first: requirements gathering, stakeholder alignment, scope boundaries, and the User Stories that came out of it.

Requirements gathering Stakeholder alignment User Stories KPI governance

Context

NovaRetail is a multi-vendor marketplace. Marketing and Finance both rely on two KPIs — customer loyalty rate and revenue — to steer their decisions. The trigger: Marketing couldn't explain why the loyalty rate was stagnant despite rising revenue. Digging into it surfaced the real issue: the two teams were computing both KPIs differently, from tables that weren't consistent with each other (duplicated customer IDs depending on the table, cancelled orders included in gross revenue).

Business need: identify at-risk customer segments and make the loyalty measurement reliable enough for both teams to act on the same numbers.

Stakeholders

Marketing

Sponsor of the request. Owns the loyalty rate KPI and the segmentation use case.

Finance

Validates the revenue figures. Needed to agree on gross vs. net-of-cancellations revenue.

Data Engineering

Responsible for source reliability — the duplicated customer ID issue traced back here.

Scope

In scope

  • Clear, single definition of the loyalty KPI
  • Reliable revenue definition (handling cancelled orders)
  • Customer segmentation model for churn risk
  • Dashboard refreshed on a monthly cycle

Explicitly out of scope

  • Real-time prediction
  • Live marketplace integration

KPI governance — resolving the definitions

Both KPIs looked simple on a dashboard but depended on upstream decisions that nobody had written down. Mapping the dependencies is what made the conflict visible and gave both teams something concrete to agree on.

Revenue (North Star metric)

  • Fed by active customer count — counted via customer_unique_id, not customer_id (which duplicated customers across orders)
  • Fed by average order value
  • Sensitive to order status — gross (v1) vs. net of cancelled orders (v2), the point Finance needed resolved

Loyalty rate (supporting metric)

  • Depends on the loyalty tier definition (v1/v2, logged in a governance register)
  • Drives per-customer churn risk
  • Influences revenue over time

From requirements to User Stories

Once the definitions were agreed, they translated directly into stories the data team could build against — each with acceptance criteria precise enough to test.

US-01
As a Marketing analyst, I want each customer counted once regardless of how many order records they appear in, so that the loyalty rate isn't artificially inflated.
Given two orders placed under different customer_id but the same customer_unique_id
When the active customer count is computed
Then that customer is counted once, not twice
US-02
As a Finance stakeholder, I want cancelled orders excluded from the net revenue figure, so that revenue reporting reflects completed transactions only.
Given an order with status = "canceled"
When monthly net revenue (v2) is calculated
Then that order's amount is excluded from the total
US-03
As a Marketing sponsor, I want a loyalty segmentation dashboard that refreshes monthly, so that I can spot at-risk segments without manually reconciling two teams' spreadsheets.
Given the monthly data pipeline run has completed
When the Marketing team opens the Tableau dashboard
Then loyalty segments and churn indicators reflect the latest closed month

Outcome

2.72%

revenue discrepancy identified between the gross and net-of-cancellations definitions once the scope above was built on Snowflake (dbt, automated tests) across ~99,400 orders — the exact gap Finance and Marketing had been arguing past each other about.
