Functional analysis case study

NovaRetail KPI Governance — Scoping two conflicting metrics

Before writing a single dbt model, the real problem was that Marketing and Finance didn't agree on what "revenue" and "loyal customer" meant. Here's the requirements work that came first: stakeholder alignment, scope boundaries, and the User Stories that came out of it.

Context & business need

NovaRetail is a multi-vendor marketplace. Marketing couldn't explain why the loyalty rate was stagnant despite rising revenue. Digging in surfaced the real issue: both teams computed revenue and loyalty differently, from tables that weren't consistent with each other (duplicated customer IDs depending on the table, cancelled orders included in gross revenue). The need: make both KPIs reliable enough for everyone to act on the same numbers.

Stakeholders

Marketing

Sponsor of the request. Owns the loyalty rate KPI and segmentation use case.

Finance

Validates revenue figures. Needed alignment on gross vs. net-of-cancellations revenue.

Data Engineering

Responsible for source reliability — the duplicated customer ID issue traced back here.

Scope

In scope

  • Single definition of the loyalty KPI
  • Reliable revenue definition (handling cancellations)
  • Customer segmentation for churn risk
  • Dashboard refreshed monthly

Explicitly out of scope

  • Real-time prediction
  • Live marketplace integration

KPI governance — resolving the definitions

Revenue (North Star metric)

  • Fed by active customer count — via customer_unique_id, not customer_id (which duplicated customers)
  • Fed by average order value
  • Sensitive to order status — gross (v1) vs. net of cancellations (v2)

Loyalty rate (supporting metric)

  • Depends on the loyalty tier definition (logged in a governance register)
  • Drives per-customer churn risk
  • Influences revenue over time

From requirements to User Stories

US-01
As a Marketing analyst, I want each customer counted once regardless of how many order records they appear in, so that the loyalty rate isn't artificially inflated.
Given two orders placed under different customer_id but the same customer_unique_id
When the active customer count is computed
Then that customer is counted once, not twice
US-02
As a Finance stakeholder, I want cancelled orders excluded from the net revenue figure, so that revenue reporting reflects completed transactions only.
Given an order with status = "canceled"
When monthly net revenue (v2) is calculated
Then that order's amount is excluded from the total
US-03
As a Marketing sponsor, I want a loyalty segmentation dashboard that refreshes monthly, so that I can spot at-risk segments without manually reconciling two teams' spreadsheets.
Given the monthly data pipeline run has completed
When the Marketing team opens the Tableau dashboard
Then loyalty segments and churn indicators reflect the latest closed month

Outcome

2.72%

revenue discrepancy identified between the gross and net-of-cancellations definitions once this scope was built on Snowflake (dbt, automated tests) across ~99,400 orders — the exact gap Finance and Marketing had been arguing past each other about.

Investigation case study

Hotel Booking Demand — From a cancellation question to a targeted recommendation

No stakeholder conflict to resolve here — this one is a straight investigation: narrowing a vague "cancellations are too high" concern down to 15 named agents, while handling a real data quality issue along the way.

Business question

The overall cancellation rate across 119,390 reservations is 37%. Which segments concentrate the risk, and what action would recover the most revenue?

Investigation path

  • Groups segment — cancellation rate of 61%, nearly double other segments (13–37%); 19,557 reservations, €2.80M in lost revenue over the period
  • "Non Refund" deposits — 99.4% cancellation rate (14,590 reservations), checked against reservation_status to rule out a data artifact before trusting the number
  • Concentration by agent — 78% of Non Refund cancellations trace to just 15 agents, one alone accounting for 28%; company empty on 97% of these, so it runs through travel agencies, not corporate accounts

Data quality handled, not ignored

The children column held the literal text "NA" on 4 rows instead of a number or a blank — a direct load fails on that. Fixed by loading the column into a session variable and converting "NA" to NULL before insertion (NULLIF(@children, 'NA')), rather than dropping the rows. All 119,390 rows loaded cleanly after the fix, 0 warnings.

Recommendation — and the caveat stated up front

A partial non-refundable deposit or firm confirmation date for group bookings could recover up to €1.93M if it brought the Groups cancellation rate down to the Corporate segment's level (19%). Caveat stated explicitly: a policy perceived as too restrictive by group-booking agencies could also reduce booking volume, not just the cancellation rate — the two effects need monitoring together, not assumed independently. Recommended first step: a targeted audit of the top 5 "Non Refund" agents (46% of concentrated cancellations) rather than a blanket policy change.

From findings to User Stories

US-01
As a Revenue Manager, I want cancellation rates broken down by market segment and deposit type, so I can identify where a policy change would have real impact instead of guessing.
Given bookings tagged by market_segment and deposit_type
When the cancellation rate is computed per combination
Then combinations above a defined threshold are flagged for review
US-02
As Head of Sales for the agency channel, I want visibility into which specific agents drive Non Refund cancellations, so I can target renegotiation instead of changing policy for every partner.
Given a Non Refund booking is canceled
When the originating agent is identified
Then agents above a concentration threshold appear on a dedicated watchlist
US-03
As a Finance stakeholder, I want any revenue-recovery estimate presented with its volume-risk caveat attached, so a policy decision isn't made on the upside number alone.
Given a proposed policy change with an estimated revenue recovery
When the business case is presented
Then the potential booking-volume impact is stated alongside the recovery estimate

Outcome

€1.93M

recoverable revenue identified (~7% of total confirmed revenue) — with the audit-first, policy-second path recommended as the safer way to capture it.
